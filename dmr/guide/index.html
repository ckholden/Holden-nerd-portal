DMR PTT (phone)

A walkie-talkie in your phone's browser on the family talkgroup (TGIF HOLDEN1) and more. No hotspot or radio needed. On a Windows PC, use the desktop app instead.

  1. Sign in with Google, or tap "Email me a sign-in link" for AOL, Yahoo or any other address. The first time, you request access (callsign + DMR ID) and Christian gets a push to approve it.
  2. Tap 🔌 Connect. This opens your mic and the connection. After you stop, it comes back as "▶ Resume monitoring".
  3. Pick a network tab. TGIF (home base: HOLDEN1 and our curated talkgroups) and BrandMeister always show. BrandMeister stays locked until you tap Enable BrandMeister in Settings. PNWDigital, BC-TRBO and HOLDEN (our private talkgroup) appear only once your account has them.
  4. Find a talkgroup. Tap "All talkgroups" to open the list, or type in the filter box to narrow it. Tap a name to tune to it, and tap ☆ to star a favourite. You can only pick from the list; typing a bare number no longer dials it.
  5. Talk. Hold the big PTT button and release to listen. Settings can switch it to tap-on/tap-off.
  6. Leave it open to monitor, or tap "Stop monitoring" to go quiet.
Install it as a real app, not just a pinned tab: tap 📲 Install app (Android and desktop Chrome/Edge show this automatically). On iPhone, Safari doesn't support that button, so use Share ↑ then "Add to Home Screen". iPhone requires that install step before push alerts will work at all.
  • 📟 Page (page-enabled accounts, while tuned to HOLDEN1 on TGIF): one button per person plus ALL, and an optional note ("come up on OR1"). The person gets a push on their phone and hears the page tones on HOLDEN1. There's a short cooldown between pages.
  • 📋 Radio Bulletins: Radio's on-air bulletin board. You can read it, have it read aloud, and, if you have a full account, post one by typing it in. Bulletins that mention your callsign are highlighted.
  • Listen-only access for family without a license: holdenportal.com/dmrptt/rx. It asks only for a name and can never transmit. Listeners show with 🎧 in the online list.
  • Status: "Reconnecting…" means it lost the connection and is retrying on its own. "Bridge offline" means the server side of that network is down, so talking is disabled until it's back.
  • A yellow ⚠ PATCHED banner means HOLDEN DMR is currently linked to TGIF HOLDEN1. See HOLDEN DMR.

Settings (gear icon): PTT mode, transmit time-out, permit tone / roger beep, keep screen awake, headset mode (louder, no echo cancel), mic & speaker pickers, mic level, 🔔 Call alerts (turns on push notifications), 📵 Away alerts (whether those pushes fire while you're away; on by default), and Enable BrandMeister / Update BrandMeister password.

Holden DMR (desktop)

The Windows app: download it from the DMR page ("Holden DMR for Windows"). It updates itself. Same networks and same server as the phone app, plus a chat room, console tiles and a live map. Sign in by picking who's operating (KJ7DTS, KK7ION or someone else) with Google.

Talking

  • Your PTT key (Space unless you change it in Settings): hold to talk, release to listen. Or hold the on-screen PTT button.
  • Foot pedal (F24): works everywhere, even while you type in chat, and while another program is in front (installed app with the F24 hook). Space, Right-Ctrl, Right-Shift and a remapped key work only when you're not typing in a text box. You can turn spacebar PTT off on one computer (Settings → Controls).
  • Ctrl+Alt+Space (off until you tick it in Settings → Controls): press-on / press-off from any program.
  • It won't transmit while a network is still connecting or someone else is talking.

Networks, talkgroups and settings

  • Buttons for TGIF, BM and PNW always show; BCT (BC-TRBO) and HOLDEN show when your account has them. They're also in the Radio menu.
  • Click a talkgroup to tune. Right-click to favourite it; Shift+right-click hides a built-in one ("Show all again" in Settings brings them back).
  • Settings tabs: Audio, Talkgroups (add your own per network, hide built-ins), Console (four tiles, each with its own network/talkgroup, SELECT, PTT and an RX light), Controls, Networks (status only).
  • View menu: DMR Monitor (map), Radio bulletins, Chat panel, Night mode (remembered; the map window follows it).

Radio (the voice assistant)

Radio is an AI assistant that lives on TGIF HOLDEN1. It stays silent unless you call it by name, and it only answers licensed family operators (KJ7DTS, KK7ION). It works the same whether you're on a radio, the phone app or the desktop app.

How to talk to it

  • Start your transmission with "Radio," then ask, all in one over: "Radio, what's the weather at home?" Saying "radio" in the middle of a sentence doesn't wake it.
  • Just "Radio." checks it's there. It answers "Go ahead with your question," and your next transmission within about 20 seconds doesn't need "Radio" in front.
  • If Radio asks you something, you have about 30 seconds to answer without saying "Radio".
  • Talk for at least a second; very short blips are ignored. Radio can't hear you while it's talking, so wait until it finishes.
  • There's no "stand by". A question that needs a lookup just takes a few seconds longer before the answer starts.
  • It identifies itself as KJ7DTS as the rules require, and it stops itself after 90 seconds of talking.

The basics

Your radio does two things: ANALOG (FM) for regular ham repeaters & simplex, and DMR (digital) for the PNWDigital network, where your local repeater is linked to repeaters across Oregon and Washington so you can talk a long way, not just line-of-sight. Channels are grouped into zones (think folders) like "DMR POWELLBTT" or "HAM CROOK CO".

Calling family on DMR

  1. Everyone tunes to their OR1 channel (e.g. Pete: PB OR1, Chris: CORB OR1), the statewide calling talkgroup.
  2. Call: "KK7RBQ, this is KJ7DTS, on Oregon 1, Powell Butte."
  3. They answer with their callsign.
  4. Move off the shared calling channel: "Let's go to TAC 310." Both switch to the TAC310 channel (e.g. PB TAC310 / CORB TAC310).
  5. Chat as long as you like. ID with your callsign at the start, at the end, and every ~10 minutes.

Analog works the same way but with no switching: call and chat right on the one repeater channel. Through a hotspot, the family channels are TGIF HOLDEN1 and our private HOLDEN DMR.

Understanding DMR talkgroups

Picture a DMR repeater as a highway with two lanes (timeslots). Each lane carries different conversations (talkgroups): "Local 1", "Oregon 1", "PNW 1" and so on. Your channel is parked on one talkgroup. The #1 gotcha: you can hear someone on a different talkgroup from the one your channel is set to, but if you key up, they won't hear you. Fix: check what talkgroup the screen shows for the incoming call, switch to the channel for that talkgroup, then key up.

How DMR networking reaches so far

An old-school analog repeater only covers its own local radio footprint, usually 20-50 miles. DMR repeaters can do that too, but they can also be linked together over the internet into a network. Every DMR repeater (and every personal "hotspot", which is really a tiny internet-connected repeater sitting on your desk) is plugged into a network server: PNWDigital, BrandMeister, TGIF, or our own private one. When you key up on a networked talkgroup, your voice goes out over the air from your local repeater and over the internet to that talkgroup's server, which instantly relays it to every other repeater or hotspot tuned to the same talkgroup.

That's the whole trick: a talkgroup is really a shared internet "room". Someone across the country hears you not because your signal traveled that far, but because their nearby repeater or hotspot is plugged into the same room. It's also why the same talkgroup number can mean different things on different networks. PNWDigital and BrandMeister are separate networks with their own rooms, though a handful of talkgroups (like BRIDGE, BAYNET, SOCAL, MPRG) are bridged so the same room exists on both.

This is also why DMR Monitor can show you "who's talking" without you touching a radio: it's listening in on the same internet rooms your repeaters are plugged into.

Network etiquette

  • Listen first, and make sure the talkgroup is quiet before you call.
  • Regional talkgroups (OR1/PNW1) reach across the whole state. Announce your repeater so people can place you, then move to a TAC channel to actually visit.
  • Leave a ~2 second gap after someone stops before replying.
  • On the radio, don't key talkgroup 4000. It's a hotspot/BrandMeister disconnect command and does nothing useful on PNWDigital. (In the apps, "Disconnect / go quiet" (4000) is a normal button.)

Troubleshooting

  • I hear them but they can't hear me: you're on a different talkgroup than they are. Switch to match (see above).
  • No beep when I key / can't transmit: wrong zone/channel, or a listen-only channel (some public-safety and weather channels are receive-only).
  • Is my radio even working? Go to PARROT, talk 3 seconds, listen for the echo.
  • The HOLDEN PARROT doesn't answer: make sure your hotspot is set up for HOLDEN DMR, wait ~10 seconds after any other HOLDEN traffic, and talk for at least a second or two.
  • I'm totally lost: go to your home DMR zone, channel OR1. That's family-net home base.
  • Still stuck? Call Christian.
